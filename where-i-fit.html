Section 02 — Ramp

What the first sixty days would look like

I won't know your codebase or your customers, and I'm not going to pretend the ramp is free. This is what I'd expect to be doing while I earn context.

Weeks 1–2

Go where the work happens

Ride-alongs, support tickets, the call recordings nobody enjoys. The goal is being able to state the job the way the person doing it would state it — everything downstream depends on that.

Weeks 2–4

A check before a demo

The first thing I'd hand over is a way to tell when the system is wrong — ground truth, a test, a person who'd notice. It's what tells you whether the demo everyone likes will survive a month.

Weeks 4–8

One path all the way through

Something narrow, working end to end in production, plus a plain read on which of the remaining parts shouldn't ship yet — early enough that changing course is still cheap.

Section 03 — Standards

What I hold myself to

Less about what I'm good at, more about what I refuse to do. These are the ones I'd want to be held to by a team.

  • No shipping what I can't check

    If there's no way to tell when the output is wrong, the feature isn't ready, regardless of how good the demo looked.

  • Bad news travels early

    The date is at risk, the approach isn't working, the thing you asked for isn't the thing you need — all of it is cheaper to hear in week two than week ten.

  • Leave it runnable without me

    Documented, handed over, understood by someone else. A system only I can operate is a liability I created.

  • Say what a model shouldn't do

    Part of the job is arguing against automating something. I'd rather lose that argument out loud than let it ship quietly.

  • Measure by what's still running

    Not what launched. What's still working three months later, with the original people using it.

  • Learn the domain properly

    Unfamiliar fields are the normal condition of this work, not an obstacle to it. The obligation is to get to real understanding fast, not to fake it convincingly.

Section 04 — Limits

Where I'm short, as of today

Marked plainly, because you'd find it on day thirty anyway and I'd rather you find it now.

  • Not a research engineer. My depth is applied — putting models to work — not training or evaluating them at the frontier.
  • Haven't operated at very large scale. One live system with real money in it, not millions of users.
  • I've built alone. I want the review culture of a strong team, and I expect month one to cost me some ego.
  • My practice with the current generation of agentic tooling is measured in months, not years — daily and deliberate, but recent.
