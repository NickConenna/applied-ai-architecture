Land development work

Clearing to finished — one site, start to end

All of this is the same job, in sequence: a rhododendron-choked roadside embankment at 215 Edmisten Rd, Blowing Rock, NC, cleared and cut into a vehicle-passable access road and camp bench, ending in a built utility structure and a set-up expedition tent. Every stage below was run solo — no subcontracted excavation.

Stage 1 · Clearing the cut

Bobcat E32 working a rhododendron thicket on the roadside embankment — root balls and brush cleared to open the line for the access cut.

Stage 2 · Grading the pad

Same cut, graded to a flat, driveable bench — the SUV parked on it is the proof of load-bearing, compacted surface.

Stage 3 · Cutting the access trail

Pushing the road further up-slope through fog and root mass — the messiest, least photogenic part of the job, included on purpose.

Stage 4 · Finished access road

Compacted, crowned, and holding — plus a framed-and-sided utility structure built alongside it, on-site carpentry beyond just excavation.

Stage 5 · Graded bench, drainage cut

Swales cut into the bench to route water off the pad before anything gets built on it — planned before it was needed, not after.

Stage 6 · Access down to the site

Reclaimed-pallet steps down the slope to the finished bench — camp seating and the tent already in place below.

Stage 7 · Finished

The end state: a canvas wall tent on a built platform, on ground that was rhododendron thicket a season earlier.
