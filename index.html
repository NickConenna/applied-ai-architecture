Method / 02

Context × Loops

AI projects die in one of two places: a discovery phase that produces a document, or a demo that works on the happy path and collapses on real data.

Context. Before anything gets built, the system is loaded with the real material — actual records, actual constraints, the exceptions everyone knows about and nobody wrote down. Most implementations underperform because they were built against a description of the work rather than the work itself.

Loops. Then tight build–test–correct cycles against live conditions. Not one specification followed by one build. Small increments, each checked against reality, each correction feeding the next. The loop is where the specification actually gets written — by the work, not before it.

Why 70%. There's enough to start at about seventy percent of the information. The last thirty percent isn't sitting in a stakeholder's head waiting to be extracted — it doesn't exist yet, and it gets found faster by building than by asking. Waiting for certainty is how a four-week project becomes a four-month one that ships something nobody wanted.

Moving at 70% only works if someone carries the risk of being wrong. On a fixed fee against a fixed date, that's me. If the loop runs slow, it's my cost, not yours.
