Book I · The Pace

The premise

He can measure anything except people

A metrologist tells the time without a clock and the distance without a tape. He knows the circumference of the earth in breaths, dates a building by the unit it was built to, and can say how long he has been unconscious to the minute. He has never once been able to read a man across a table.

His stepfather has just been murdered at Wardenclyffe — and spent the last eleven minutes of his life pacing a deliberate line across a field, because there is exactly one person alive who would look at a body in the weeds and ask the correct question.

Not what killed him. How far did he walk.

Before you begin

Fact

  • For 130 years the kilogram was a cylinder of platinum-iridium in a vault at Sèvres, behind three keys held by three separate parties. It drifted. In 2019 it was retired and redefined against the Planck constant.
  • In 1834 the Palace of Westminster burned, destroying the imperial standards of the yard and the pound. Replacements took more than a decade. For those years the yard did not exist — and almost nobody noticed.
  • In 1901 J.P. Morgan financed the tower at Wardenclyffe believing he was buying wireless telegraphy. When he understood Tesla intended to transmit power instead, he withdrew and never wrote another cheque.
  • A circle holds 21,600 arcminutes. A nautical mile is one arcminute of latitude. The earth is 21,600 nautical miles around. A human day, in the yogic count, is 21,600 breaths.
  • In November 1910, six men travelled to Jekyll Island using only their first names, behind a duck-hunting cover story, to draft what became the Federal Reserve.

All of the above is true

The opening

The Pace — Prologue

Wardenclyffe · Shoreham, New York · 4:11 a.m.

Dr. Ambrose Teague had eleven minutes left and he intended to spend them walking.

The brick laboratory behind him was the last building Stanford White designed before a jealous husband shot him in the face at Madison Square Garden. Teague had always thought that was the honest epigraph for this place. Everything here had ended badly and none of it had been wrong.

The tower was gone — sold for scrap in 1917 against a hotel bill. But the foundation remained under the weeds. An octagon of concrete, and beneath it the shaft, sunk a hundred and twenty feet into Long Island sand, because Tesla had insisted that to grip the earth you must first get your hand around it.

Behind him, the woman was still coming. She had not hurried once.

That was the detail he could not stop noticing, even with his shirt going warm and heavy against his ribs. She walked at a fixed rate. She had walked at that rate through the parking lot, through the treeline, through the twenty minutes it took her to find him, and she was walking at it still. A metronome inside a person. Teague had spent forty-one years measuring things and he knew a calibrated instrument when he saw one.

"Doctor." Her voice was pleasant. "You've made your point. Where is it?"

He did not answer. He put his left foot down, then his right, and he counted.

What it's about

The argument

A measure taken from the body, against a measure imposed on it

The cubit is the largest human-scaled unit. The quark is the floor of matter. Everything between them is contested territory.

Most arguments that look like they are about money are actually about measurement. Whoever defines the unit is usually not in the room, and has usually already won.

The antagonist

He is right about something, and that is the problem

He watched his grandmother paper a kitchen wall with banknotes in 1923. He concluded, before he was nine, that somebody must be responsible for the ruler.

A measure without a keeper is not free. It is unguarded. He is never refuted in these books. He is only outvoted.

The wound

A man who is never late, and thirty years too late

Greek carried two words for time. Chronos is countable and sequential — his entire life. Kairos is the appointed moment, which arrives on no schedule and cannot be produced by counting.

He has mastered one and has never once been on time for the other.

The instruments

Every unit here came off a human body

The reader is not required to remember any of this. The protagonist cannot forget it.

Digit
A finger's width. The smallest unit anyone ever needed, and the one you always have with you.
Palm
Four digits. Horses are still measured in hands, which is the same idea wearing a different hat.
Span
Thumb-tip to little-finger-tip on a spread hand. Half a cubit, near enough, on most men.
Cubit
Elbow to fingertip. The largest unit that is still a person. The Great Pyramid is built in these.
Foot
A foot. Twelve inches because twelve divides cleanly by two, three, four and six, and ten does not.
Pace
A double step. Mille passus — a thousand of them — is the Roman mile, and therefore the American one.
Fathom
Both arms outstretched. From fæthm, an embrace. The sea was measured in hugs.
Canna
The canna napoletana — eight palms, the working measure of the Kingdom of Naples, and so of Bari. It is also the protagonist's surname.

How these are made

I write with AI. Not have an AI write it — the distinction matters and I draw it precisely in the author's note. I bring the obsessions, the family material, the thematic commitments, and the veto. The collaboration brings research, structure, drafting at volume, and persistent argument.

Every historical claim in these books is checkable, and a historical note in each volume separates what is real from what is invented. Under current U.S. Copyright Office guidance, AI-generated material is not itself copyrightable and registration requires disclosure. That disclosure is made.

I would rather say so on page one than be asked about it later.
