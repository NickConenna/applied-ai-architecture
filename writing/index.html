The books

A four-book series

The Cubit and the Quark

A metrologist can tell you the circumference of the earth in breaths. He can date a building by the unit it was built to. He cannot read a man across a table — and his stepfather has just been shot at the foot of Tesla's tower on Long Island, having spent the last eleven minutes of his life walking a deliberate sixty-eight paces across a field. The distance is the message.

Fact

For 130 years the kilogram was a cylinder of platinum-iridium in a vault at Sèvres, behind three keys held by three parties. In 2019 it was retired and redefined against the Planck constant.

In 1834 the Palace of Westminster burned, destroying the imperial standards of the yard and the pound. Replacements took more than a decade. For those years, the yard did not exist.

In 1901 J.P. Morgan financed the tower at Wardenclyffe believing he was buying wireless telegraphy. When he understood Tesla intended to transmit power, he withdrew. You cannot sell what you cannot meter.

All of the above is true.

How these are made

I write with AI. Not have an AI write it — the distinction matters and I draw it precisely in the author's note. I bring the obsessions, the family material, the thematic commitments, and the veto. The collaboration brings research, structure, drafting at volume, and persistent argument.

Every historical claim in these books is checkable, and a historical note in each volume separates what is real from what is invented. Under current U.S. Copyright Office guidance, AI-generated material is not itself copyrightable and registration requires disclosure. That disclosure is made.
