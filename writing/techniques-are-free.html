First paragraph renders larger automatically — make it the whole argument in two or three sentences. Someone who reads only this should know what you claim and why it matters.

Body paragraphs are plain. Wrap each one in its own paragraph tag; don't use line breaks between them. Bold marks the load-bearing claim, not emphasis for its own sake.

A section heading

Sections every three to five paragraphs. Headings that state something beat headings that label something — "Discovery phases produce documents, not software" over "On discovery."

  • Lists earn their place when items are genuinely parallel
  • Three to five items; more than seven and it's prose in disguise
  • Never a list of full paragraphs
A quote from someone else, or a line from a client. Attribution goes in the following paragraph, not inside the quote.

Pull quote — one line of your own, the sentence you'd want screenshotted. One per article, maximum.

A note class for asides, caveats, and technical footnotes that would break the flow inline.

Close on the reader, not on yourself

The last paragraph should hand the reader something they can use tomorrow. Ending on a summary of what you just said is the most common way a good piece loses its landing.
