The Model

Protocol, Not Platform

Most price-comparison sites monetize by keeping the number hidden until a lead form is filled out. Fair Rate Index does the opposite on purpose — the fair-price range is the public resource. The trust that generates is the actual product; matching to a pro is what gets layered on top of it, not what gates access to it.

The Knowledge Layer

What Sits Under Each Trade

Each trade carries the same four-part structure — the part that makes this a knowledge base rather than a spreadsheet of numbers.

  • Recurring items

    The jobs that actually come up most often within a given year for that trade.

  • Key points

    The handful of facts that change a homeowner's decision — what actually matters versus what's noise.

  • Component lifespans

    How long the underlying parts and systems are expected to last, so a price makes sense against a replacement timeline.

  • The fix

    What the resolution actually involves, in plain language, tied back to the price range it justifies.

Scaling Path

From One Metro to Fifty States

Structured this way, the knowledge base doesn't need local density to be useful anywhere — which is the whole argument for treating it as a national protocol instead of a metro-by-metro rollout.

Pilot

Lake Norman, NC

Where the pricing model and the eight-trade structure were built and validated first, against real jobs.

Rearchitecture

Internet-only protocol

Rebuilt to run without a physical or metro constraint — machine-readable, sponsorship- or pricing-monetized, usable the same way in any state.

National

All 50 states

The stated target — a single reference layer for fair home and auto pricing, structured for AI-augmented consumer education everywhere, not just where the founder happens to live.

Live Now

Fair Rate Index is running at fairrateindex.com today, not a concept page — the knowledge layer, the pricing structure, and the eight core trades are built and public. What's left is the state-by-state expansion.
